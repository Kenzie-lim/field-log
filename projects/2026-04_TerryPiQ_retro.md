# MS AI School Project 2: TerryPiQ — Retrospective

## What it was

MS AI School cohort 9 2nd project. A kinesiology taping
guide service — user uploads a photo, CV extracts body features,
matches the closest 3D body model from AI Hub's Korean body scan
dataset, and overlays taping recommendations generated through RAG.
Knee taping PoC. Built in roughly 2 weeks on Azure.

## What I actually did

- Technical PM
- Designed the 27-document system across 4 tiers
- Designed the 4-act presentation narrative (Conditions / Leap /
  Proof / Record), built the deck, produced the concept and demo
  videos
- Ran research across the stack — architecture patterns, AI Hub
  dataset structure, RAI principle conflicts, RAG
- Maintained the Integration Status Tracker
- Authored the OutlierPolicy and the architecture narrative
  diagrams (v1 bottleneck → v2 Brain/Delivery split)

## What I'm proud of

**The doc system and the presentation narrative came from the same
instinct.** Giving a complex thing a structure that 6 people, and
an audience of 30, could navigate without losing the thread.

**Reframing the team from "AI beginners" to a domain-embedded
team.** Two of us had deep domain knowledge — sports coaching and
3D. Naming that out loud changed where we stood.

## What I struggled with

**Letting go of the technical detail.** The pull to verify every
module myself was strong. I had to consciously shift — hand the
depth to the people doing the work, and lead by mapping context
and finding gaps instead. I'm still not sure the doc system wasn't
over-engineering. The work shipped, so I'll take it.

## What I'd do differently

**Make the document the start of a conversation, not the end of
one.** I'd write up the research and ship it, but rarely sat down
one-on-one to walk someone through it. Documents alone hid how
differently people were reading the same thing.

## What I took away

- The people. People I came to love.
- A clearer sense of what a Technical PM identity is — built in
  Q&A, not in design docs
- The compound effect of one-on-ones: the more I prepared with
  each specialist, the more confident I became in my own work
- That stopping is sometimes the work
